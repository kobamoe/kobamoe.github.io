About Me

Training as an engineering problem: a decade of systematic optimization.

Triathlon training is an engineering problem. Start with zero domain knowledge and minimal athletic background. Introduce constraints: full-time work, family obligations, limited training hours. Apply the same structured methodology used in professional engineering — hypothesis testing, data collection, iterative refinement. The result: three full-distance finishes, sub 9:00 at Ironman Copenhagen in 2023, and 3rd age-group at Ostseeman in 2024 — all measured, documented, and repeatable.

By day, I work as a full-time engineer with a PhD in applied research. By training plan, I apply the same rigorous methodology to athletics — forming hypotheses around pacing, volume, and periodization, testing them against real session data, and letting measurable outcomes drive iteration. Every training decision is grounded in logged data, not intuition. Every plan is built to be adjusted when the real world — your job, your schedule, your energy levels — changes.

Coaching is offered in English, German, and Japanese, based in Mönchengladbach, Germany, and delivered fully online to age-group athletes across Europe.

Engineering background

Structured problem-solving isn't just the day job — it's the same lens applied to training. Form a hypothesis, test it in a real session, and let the data (heart rate, pace, power, how the body actually responds) decide what changes in the next block. It's the same iterative process behind the engineering work, just pointed at swim sets and long rides instead of systems and specs.
